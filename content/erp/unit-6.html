1.1 Introduction and Meaning of SAP

SAP stands for Systems, Applications and Products in Data Processing. It is a German enterprise software company best known for developing integrated business software, including Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems.

SAP ERP helps an organisation manage important business activities—such as finance, purchasing, inventory, production, sales, human resources and maintenance—through connected applications and shared business data.

Exam definition: SAP is an integrated enterprise software solution that connects different functional areas of an organisation and helps manage business processes and information through a common system.

In simple words, SAP connects departments so that information entered in one department can be used by other authorised departments. This reduces repeated data entry and helps managers work with consistent information.

1.2 History and Development of SAP

SAP was founded on 1 April 1972 by five former IBM employees: Dietmar Hopp, Hasso Plattner, Claus Wellenreuther, Klaus Tschira and Hans-Werner Hector. The original company name was Systemanalyse Programmentwicklung, meaning System Analysis and Program Development. Its original idea was to develop standard business software that could integrate business processes and process data in real time.

1.3 SAP ERP Architecture and Integration

Traditional SAP R/3 is commonly explained through a three-tier client-server architecture:

  1. Presentation layer: The user interface through which employees enter data and view reports.
  2. Application layer: Executes business rules, validates transactions and processes application logic.
  3. Database layer: Stores business data such as customer records, material information, orders and financial transactions.

Because the modules share data, a transaction in one department can affect another department. For example, when a sales order is created, the system can check inventory, trigger delivery activities and support billing and accounting.

1.5 Important Features of SAP

  1. Integration: Connects business functions so information can flow between departments.
  2. Centralised data: Maintains common business records and reduces duplicate data.
  3. Real-time processing: Updates relevant information when transactions are posted.
  4. Modular structure: Organisations can implement functional modules according to their requirements.
  5. Standard business processes: Provides structured workflows for common business activities.
  6. Reporting and analysis: Helps users monitor operations and support decisions.
  7. Scalability and international support: Supports complex organisations, multiple locations and different currencies, depending on the product and configuration.
  8. Security and authorisation: Access can be controlled according to user roles and responsibilities.

1.6 Advantages of SAP

  • Improves coordination among departments.
  • Reduces duplicate data entry and inconsistent records.
  • Provides timely information for operational and management decisions.
  • Improves control over purchasing, inventory, production and finance.
  • Supports standardisation of business processes.
  • Helps monitor costs, resources and organisational performance.
  • Can support large, multi-location and international businesses.

1.7 Limitations / Challenges of SAP

  • High cost: Licensing, implementation, infrastructure, support and training can require substantial investment.
  • Complex implementation: Large projects need careful planning, configuration and testing.
  • Training requirement: Employees need training to use the system correctly.
  • Change management: Existing processes and employee responsibilities may need to change.
  • Dependence on data quality: Incorrect master data can lead to incorrect planning and reports.
  • Customisation challenges: Excessive customisation can increase cost and make future upgrades more difficult.

1.8 Practical Example of SAP

Consider a company that manufactures refrigerators. A customer places an order through the sales department. SAP SD records the sales order. The system checks stock information maintained through MM. If stock is insufficient, PP can help plan production. MM supports the purchase of required raw materials. When goods are delivered, SD supports delivery and billing, while FI records the financial impact. Thus, several departments work on connected information rather than maintaining separate records.

2.1 Introduction and Meaning of BAAN

BAAN refers to the ERP software originally developed by the Dutch software company Baan Corporation. It was designed to integrate business functions, with particular strength in manufacturing and complex industrial operations.

Exam definition: BAAN is an integrated ERP software package originally developed by Baan Corporation of the Netherlands. It supports major business functions such as manufacturing, finance, project management and distribution through connected applications and shared information.

BAAN became known for supporting manufacturing organisations that needed to coordinate product structures, production planning, shop-floor activities, inventory, purchasing, finance and distribution.

2.2 History and Development of BAAN

Baan Corporation was founded in the Netherlands in 1978. The BAAN ERP product family grew into a recognised enterprise software solution, particularly for discrete manufacturing and project-driven industries.

2.4 Important Functions of BAAN Manufacturing

The manufacturing component is a major area of BAAN ERP. Important functions include:

  1. Bill of Materials (BOM): Maintains the list of raw materials, parts and quantities needed to manufacture a product.
  2. Product configuration: Supports the selection of product options and variants according to customer requirements.
  3. Production planning: Plans what to manufacture, how much to manufacture and when production should take place.
  4. Production control: Monitors manufacturing orders and production activities.
  5. Routings: Defines the sequence of operations and work centres required to manufacture a product.
  6. Shop-floor control: Supports monitoring and control of activities carried out on the factory floor.
  7. Material Requirements Planning (MRP): Helps calculate material requirements based on production plans and product structures.
  8. Capacity Requirements Planning (CRP): Helps check whether sufficient machine and labour capacity is available.
  9. Engineering data and change control: Supports product information and management of engineering changes.
  10. Cost calculation: Helps calculate product and manufacturing costs.

2.5 Dynamic Enterprise Modeler (DEM)

Dynamic Enterprise Modeler (DEM) is an important concept associated with BAAN ERP. It is used to model an organisation's business structure and processes. It helps represent how activities, departments, roles and information flows are related.

  • It supports the modelling of business processes.
  • It helps describe organisational structures and responsibilities.
  • It can assist in aligning ERP configuration with business procedures.
  • It provides a structured view of how business activities are connected.
Simple example: In a manufacturing company, DEM can be used to represent the flow from customer order to planning, purchasing, production, warehousing and delivery. This makes the relationships between activities easier to understand and configure.

2.6 Working of BAAN ERP — Example Process

Consider a company manufacturing industrial pumps. When a customer order is received, the system can support a connected sequence of activities:

Customer order→Sales / Distribution→Production planning→Purchasing & inventory→Manufacturing→Warehousing & delivery→Finance
  1. The sales or distribution function records the customer requirement.
  2. Production planning checks the product structure, required quantity and schedule.
  3. Inventory records are checked to identify available materials.
  4. Purchasing arranges the materials that are not available.
  5. Manufacturing uses BOMs and routings to plan and control production.
  6. Finished goods are recorded in warehousing and delivered to the customer.
  7. Finance records relevant costs, invoices, receipts and payments.

This integrated flow reduces the need for separate, disconnected records in each department.

3.1 Introduction and Meaning of Oracle Corporation

Oracle Corporation is an American technology company known for database software, enterprise applications and cloud infrastructure. In the ERP market, Oracle provides business applications that help organisations manage finance, procurement, projects, supply chains, manufacturing, human resources and other operations.

Oracle is both a technology company and an ERP software vendor. Its ERP offerings have included Oracle Applications / Oracle E-Business Suite (EBS) and the newer Oracle Fusion Cloud ERP service. These products help connect business functions and make information available to authorised users across departments.

Exam definition: Oracle Corporation is a global enterprise-technology company that provides database systems and integrated business applications, including ERP solutions, to manage and coordinate the major functions of an organisation.

Remember: Oracle Corporation is the company/vendor; Oracle E-Business Suite and Oracle Fusion Cloud ERP are examples of its enterprise application products. Do not use these names as if they all mean exactly the same thing.

The company was established in 1977 as Software Development Laboratories (SDL) by Larry Ellison, Bob Miner and Ed Oates. It later adopted the Oracle name. Oracle expanded from database software into a wider range of enterprise applications and cloud services.

3.5 Three-Tier Architecture of Oracle E-Business Suite

Oracle E-Business Suite uses a three-tier architecture. A tier is a logical layer that performs a particular role. This separation helps organise user access, application processing and data storage.

  1. Desktop / Client Tier: Provides the user interface, generally through a web browser. Users enter transactions and request information.
  2. Application Tier: Runs application services and business logic. It processes requests, applies business rules and communicates with the database. Concurrent processing can handle background or data-intensive jobs.
  3. Database Tier: Stores and manages business data, including transactions, master records and application information.
Easy memory trick: Client tier = user interaction; Application tier = processing and business rules; Database tier = data storage and retrieval.

Oracle's official E-Business Suite architecture documentation describes the desktop/client, application and database tiers. The exact services inside the application tier depend on the release and deployment.

3.9 Practical Example — Oracle ERP in a Manufacturing Company

4.1 Introduction and Meaning of PeopleSoft

PeopleSoft is an enterprise application software product family originally developed by PeopleSoft, Inc. It provides integrated applications for managing organisational functions such as human resources, payroll, finance, supply chain, customer relationship management and higher-education administration.

PeopleSoft became part of Oracle after Oracle acquired PeopleSoft in 2005. Oracle continues to provide PeopleSoft applications and documentation, including Human Capital Management (HCM), Financials and Supply Chain Management (FSCM), Campus Solutions and PeopleTools.

Exam definition: PeopleSoft is an integrated enterprise application suite, now owned by Oracle, that supports organisational processes such as human resource management, finance, supply chain management, customer relationship management and campus administration.

PeopleSoft is especially associated with large organisations, public-sector institutions, universities and service-based organisations that require detailed HR, financial and administrative processes.

4.2 History and Development of PeopleSoft

PeopleSoft, Inc. was founded in 1987 by Dave Duffield and Ken Morris. The company developed enterprise software with a strong focus on human resources and financial applications. It expanded its product range into supply chain, customer relationship management and higher-education administration. Oracle acquired PeopleSoft in 2005, and the products became part of Oracle's enterprise applications portfolio.

4.4 PeopleTools

PeopleTools is the technology platform used to develop, configure, administer and integrate PeopleSoft applications. It provides the underlying tools and services that support the application suite.

Important PeopleTools capabilities include:

  • Application Designer: Used by developers to create and maintain application definitions and objects.
  • PeopleCode: PeopleSoft's programming language, used to implement application logic and validations.
  • Process Scheduler: Runs scheduled and background processes, such as reports and batch jobs.
  • Integration Broker: Supports communication and integration between PeopleSoft and external systems.
  • Security administration: Supports user profiles, roles, permission lists and access management.
  • Portal and user interface tools: Help users access application pages and business processes through a common interface.
Remember: PeopleSoft applications provide business functions; PeopleTools provides much of the technical platform used to build, run, administer and integrate those applications.

4.5 PeopleSoft Internet Architecture (PIA)

PeopleSoft Internet Architecture (PIA) is the web-based architecture through which users access PeopleSoft applications. It uses multiple server layers to receive user requests, process application logic and store information in the database. A simplified view includes the browser/client, web server, application server and database server.

  1. Browser / Client: The user opens PeopleSoft through a web browser and enters information or requests a service.
  2. Web Server: Receives the web request and forwards it to the appropriate application services.
  3. Application Server: Runs PeopleSoft application logic, processes requests and communicates with the database.
  4. Database Server: Stores application data, definitions and transaction records and returns requested information.

This is a simplified conceptual diagram for examination. Actual PeopleSoft deployments can include additional components, process schedulers, integration services, load balancers and other infrastructure depending on PeopleTools version and configuration.

4.6 Important Features of PeopleSoft

  • Integrated application suite: Connects HR, finance, supply chain, campus and customer-related processes.
  • Strong HCM capabilities: Supports employee administration, payroll, benefits, time and labour and workforce processes.
  • Financial management: Supports accounting, payables, receivables, assets and related financial processes.
  • Higher-education support: Campus Solutions provides functions for student and academic administration.
  • Web-based access: PIA enables browser-based access to PeopleSoft applications.
  • PeopleTools platform: Provides development, administration, workflow and integration capabilities.
  • Role-based security: Helps control which users can view or perform particular actions.
  • Configurable business processes: Supports organisational rules and workflows.

4.7 Advantages of PeopleSoft

  1. Centralised employee information: HR departments can maintain employee data in an integrated system.
  2. Efficient HR administration: Helps manage recruitment, payroll, benefits, leave and workforce records.
  3. Integrated finance and supply chain: Connects financial transactions with purchasing and other operational activities.
  4. Useful for universities: Campus Solutions supports admissions, student records and academic administration.
  5. Improved reporting: Provides information for administrative and management decisions.
  6. Web-based access: Users can access applications through a browser-based interface, subject to security and system configuration.
  7. Customisation and integration: PeopleTools supports development and connections with other enterprise systems.

4.8 Limitations / Challenges of PeopleSoft

  • Implementation cost: Deployment, configuration, consulting, infrastructure and training can be expensive.
  • Complexity: Large installations can involve many modules, users, workflows and integrations.
  • Training: Users and administrators require suitable training.
  • Customisation maintenance: Extensive modifications may increase the effort required for upgrades and support.
  • Data migration: Moving employee, financial or student records from legacy systems requires careful validation.
  • Integration effort: Connecting PeopleSoft with other systems may require technical planning and ongoing maintenance.
  • Change management: Organisations must adapt their procedures and help employees adopt the system.

4.9 Practical Example — PeopleSoft in a University

Suppose a university uses PeopleSoft Campus Solutions and related enterprise applications. An applicant submits an admission form. The university records the application, verifies documents and updates the applicant's status. Once admitted, the student receives a student record and can register for courses. Student financials can record tuition fees and payments. HCM manages faculty and staff information, while Financials and Supply Chain Management support purchasing, payments and institutional accounting.

This example demonstrates how PeopleSoft can support both student-facing and administrative processes in a higher-education institution.

4.10 PeopleSoft — Exam-Ready Short Note (5–7.5 marks)

PeopleSoft is an integrated enterprise application software family originally developed by PeopleSoft, Inc., which was founded in 1987 by Dave Duffield and Ken Morris. Oracle acquired PeopleSoft in 2005, and it became part of Oracle's enterprise applications portfolio.

PeopleSoft provides applications for Human Capital Management (HCM), Financials and Supply Chain Management (FSCM), Campus Solutions, Customer Relationship Management (CRM) and Enterprise Performance Management (EPM). HCM manages employee-related activities, FSCM supports financial and supply chain processes, and Campus Solutions supports university administration.

An important technology platform is PeopleTools, which supports application development, configuration, administration and integration. PeopleSoft Internet Architecture (PIA) provides browser-based access through a web server, application server and database server.

Major advantages include integrated HR and finance processes, improved employee and student administration, reporting, web-based access and customisation. Limitations include implementation cost, complexity, training, data migration and maintenance of customisations.

Conclusion: PeopleSoft is an important enterprise application suite, particularly for organisations requiring extensive HR, financial, supply chain and higher-education administration.
